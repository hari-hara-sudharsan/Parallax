For every second *t* in the window: if a stop triggered at *t* and filled *L* seconds later, how far had the price fallen? `cost = (P(t) − P(t+L)) / P(t)`, in basis points (1 bp = $10 on a $100k position). Negative means the price had bounced. Percentiles are nearest-rank, so every value is one that occurred.

## How to read this

- **The tail is the point.** On an average second, fall and bounce largely cancel, so averages are small. A stop exists for the bad seconds: the p95 column is what a slow fill costs exactly when protection matters.
- The cost is signed (a bounce counts in your favour), which makes these numbers conservative compared with measuring the largest move inside the wait.
- These are exchange price moves, not our Uniswap v4 pool. They measure how far the market runs while a stop waits to fill; a thin pool would gap further.
- 1.4 s and 2.0 s are measured (`results/SUMMARY.md`, keeper baseline). 4.4 s and 7.4 s are **derived**: our measured loop with Lasna's ~1 s block replaced by a ~7 s legacy block (on average a transaction waits half a block, so +3 s; at worst a full block, +6 s). Nothing was run on legacy Reactive.
- Prices between 1-second closes are linearly interpolated.
